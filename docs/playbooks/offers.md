## 1. The Value Equation (why people buy)

Perceived value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) / (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)

Raise the top, shrink the bottom. Every offer edit should name which
variable it moves.

**AceVolumes applied:** our buyers (US lenders, small businesses) buy the
dream outcome "a website that wins trust and generates leads while you run
the business", high likelihood ("100+ sites built since 2016, named
case studies"), low time ("launch weeks, not months"), low effort ("logo
files + photos + 1 hour of your time — we do the rest", already on the
public site). The equation is why the "deliberately small studio" story
works: founder-built = likelihood up.

## 2. Grand Slam Offer, 5 steps

1. **Dream outcome** — sell the destination, not the vehicle ("clients who
   trust you before the first call", not "a website").
2. **List every problem** in the buyer's journey — for us: "agencies
   disappear", "my cousin's WordPress guy", "will it load fast?", "who
   actually builds it?", "what if I hate it?", "will Google find it?".
3. **Reverse each problem into a solution** — that list IS the value stack:
   founder-built, US-timezone replies ≤1 working day, sub-2s pages, weekly
   previews, revisions until right, SEO baked in from day one.
4. **Pick delivery vehicles** — 1:1 founder work (highest value), templates
   only as starting points never endings, software (mortgage OS) as the
   premium tier.
5. **Trim and stack** — name the deliverables so they feel like a pile:
   strategy session → sitemap → copy draft → design → build → launch →
   30-day tuning.

## 3. Pricing shape

- Anchor high, present 3 tiers, make the middle obvious.
- Premium is justified by guarantees that cost you nothing if you're good:
  "reply within one working day or the month is free" style commitments
  (pick ones we can 100% keep — the site already promises the reply time).
- Never discount; add scope or add a bonus instead. Bonuses must kill a
  named objection ("you'll ghost me" → weekly recorded progress updates).

## 4. Objection map (pre-empt on the page, in outreach)

| Belief | Shift we show |
|---|---|
| "It won't work for MY industry" | named case studies across 8 verticals |
| "Too expensive" | cost of a site that converts nothing; payment structure |
| "Too slow" | launch-weeks timeline, stated on the call |
| "You're far away (Lagos)" | US-first timezones, ≤1 working day, worldwide clients since 2016 |
| "Who builds it?" | David's face on the founder page; no account managers |
| "What if I hate it?" | preview checkpoints; revisions until right |

## 5. Hook types for Scribe (write 2–3 variants per post, pick best)

1. **Outcome** — "Get the client before the first call."
2. **Time** — "From brief to live in weeks, not quarters."
3. **Effort** — "One hour of your time. We build the rest."
4. **Callout** — "US lenders: your website is closing or scaring borrowers."
5. **Contrarian** — "You don't need more traffic. You need the right page."
6. **Pain** — "Your site loads in 6 seconds. Your competitor's loads in 2."
7. **Mechanism** — "The founder builds it himself. That's the feature."
8. **Transformation** — "From 'we have a site' to 'our site sells'."

Rules: specific numbers beat adjectives, lead with the outcome, make fast
and easy visible in the first line, one idea per hook, no clickbait we
can't cash.

## 6. Core Four (lead channels — matches our traffic plan)

1. **Warm outreach** (one-to-one) — the 7 client footer-credit asks, the
   LinkedIn openers. Highest conversion, do first.
2. **Published content** (one-to-many) — the Scribe/Buffer engine + case
   studies + this site's SEO.
3. **Paid traffic** — later, only after organic proof compounds.
4. **Referrals** — ask every delivered client; the footer-credit links are
   referrals in disguise.

## 7. Money models (how revenue is structured)

- Cash now: deposits on kickoff (already practice).
- Cash soon: milestone payments (design approval, launch).
- Cash lifetime: care plans / monthly retainers — the recurring line that
  makes the studio a company, not a freelance queue. Offer as "launch is
  the beginning: hosting, updates, tuning, reporting" tier.

## Use in the autonomy system

- **Scribe**: every drafted post carries 2 alternative hooks (section 5) —
  visible in the Content page edit view, swap = one click.
- **Offer Strategist (future agent)**: runs a client brief through
  sections 1–4 and drafts an offer doc as a billable deliverable.
- **Client work**: sections 1–4 are the discovery-call structure.
